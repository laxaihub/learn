*Identity & Access · Published October 11, 2026*

Imagine a school with a library, classrooms, and a staff room. A student can borrow books, but that does not mean they can open the staff room.

**IAM means Identity and Access Management.** It manages digital identities and what they are allowed to access. Identities can belong to people, apps, or other systems.

## Two questions to remember
**Authentication:** Have you proved the identity you claim? Think of showing a school ID.

**Authorization:** What are you allowed to do? Think of checking which rooms your pass can open.

Signing in successfully does not give you permission to do everything.

## A LaxAIHub example
In a possible LaxAIHub design, a learner can open an enrolled course. A contributor can edit permitted articles. An administrator can manage selected site settings. Each role gets only the access it needs.

That idea is called **least privilege**: give enough access for the task, without unnecessary permissions.

## Access should change with the job
When someone joins, changes roles, or leaves, their access needs updating. Old permissions should not stay forever.

## Try it on paper
Draw three rooms: library, classroom, and staff room. Give a student, teacher, and librarian the permissions they need. Explain your choices.

**Remember: Proving who you are and checking what you may do are different steps.**

Create a paper permission table for Learner, Contributor, and Administrator. Add “read a course,” “edit an article,” and “manage site settings.” Then change someone’s role and remove the permissions they no longer need.
