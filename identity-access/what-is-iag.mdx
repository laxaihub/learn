*Identity & Access · Published October 11, 2026*

Imagine a club that gives members keys. One member leaves, another becomes treasurer, and a third only needs a key for a weekend. Someone must keep the key list up to date.

**IAG means Identity and Access Governance** in this article. It helps an organization decide who should have access, why they need it, and when it should end. You may also see the related term **IGA: Identity Governance and Administration**, which includes administering those decisions.

## What does governance involve?
People request access, an appropriate owner approves it, and reviewers periodically check whether it is still needed. When someone changes roles or leaves, permissions should change too. Records help explain what was decided.

## A LaxAIHub example
In a possible design, a guest writer receives access to one article for a month. The editor approves the request. When the assignment ends, the access expires or is removed.

IAM manages identities and permissions. Governance adds ownership, reviews, and evidence around those decisions.

## Try it on paper
List three fictional club members, the access each needs, an owner who can approve it, and a review date. Now remove a member: what needs changing?

**Remember: Access needs a reason, an owner, and regular review.**
