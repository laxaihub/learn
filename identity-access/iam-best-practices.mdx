*Identity & Access · Published October 11, 2026*

Imagine a club where everyone shares one key and nobody knows who still has it. Digital access becomes hard to manage in much the same way.

Good IAM makes access clearer, more limited, and easier to review.

## Six practices to remember
- **Use individual accounts.** Shared accounts make responsibility harder to trace.
- **Strengthen sign-in.** Use multifactor authentication and prefer phishing-resistant methods, such as supported security keys or passkeys.
- **Limit permissions.** Give people and applications only the access their tasks need.
- **Update access promptly.** Review permissions after role changes and remove access when it is no longer needed.
- **Protect administrator roles.** Separate routine work from privileged tasks and use temporary elevation where appropriate.
- **Review logs and plan recovery.** Know how to investigate suspicious activity and restore legitimate access safely.

MFA methods provide different levels of protection. Extra sign-in checks do not replace permission checks.

## A LaxAIHub example
In a possible design, contributors edit permitted content, learners access their enrolled courses, and powerful administrator actions receive extra controls.

## Try it on paper
Audit a fictional club: find a shared account, an old member with access, and an editor with unnecessary administrator rights. Suggest a fix for each.

**Remember: Strong sign-in, limited permissions, and regular review work together.**

## 60–90 second video
Show six access habits with short labels. Focus on individual accounts, stronger sign-in, permission limits, removing old access, protected admin roles, and review.
Suggested pacing: 10 seconds for the everyday analogy, 25 seconds for the concept, 25 seconds for the access example, and 10 seconds for the takeaway.
Use large captions and slow speech. Explain acronyms before using them. Keep analogy scenes separate from computer-system scenes.

## Paper lab
Create five fictional accounts with owner, role, last review, and permissions. Identify shared, stale, and overprivileged access. Propose changes without touching a live system.
Record the request, relevant evidence, decision, and reason. No accounts, configuration changes, or paid software are needed.
