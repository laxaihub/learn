*Identity & Access · Published October 11, 2026*

Imagine a caretaker with a school’s master key. It opens many doors, so losing it could cause much more trouble than losing a classroom key.

In a computer system, powerful permissions can let someone change settings, delete data, or create accounts.

**PAM means Privileged Access Management.** It protects and controls this powerful access. PAM is a specialized part of IAM.

## What does PAM help with?
- Give only the permissions needed for a task.
- Require stronger identity checks, such as multifactor authentication: more than one kind of identity evidence.
- Where appropriate, require approval and make elevated access temporary.
- Protect powerful account credentials and record activity for review.

These controls help reduce risk; they do not guarantee that nothing will go wrong.

## A simple example
An authorized administrator needs to change one server setting. Under an example policy, they request the required role, complete extra checks, receive approval, and activate it for 30 minutes. The elevated permission then expires, and the activity is available for review.

The 30-minute limit is just an example. Organizations choose policies for their own needs.

## Try it on paper
Your club needs someone to update its website. Should they get one editing permission for an hour, or unlimited administrator access forever? Explain why.

**Remember: Powerful access deserves stronger protection and a clear purpose.**
