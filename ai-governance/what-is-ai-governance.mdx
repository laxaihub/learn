*AI Governance · Published October 11, 2026*

Imagine a club adding an AI helper. Who decides what it can do? Who checks its mistakes? Who handles a complaint?

**AI governance** is the set of responsibilities, policies, and processes that guide how an organization develops and uses AI.

## What should it cover?
Name an owner, explain the purpose, decide what data is appropriate, and identify who might be affected. Test the system, document limitations, and decide when people must review its work. Plan how to respond if something goes wrong.

Governance covers security, but also concerns such as privacy, fairness, reliability, and accountability.

## A useful framework
The NIST AI Risk Management Framework organizes work into **Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage**. These are connected activities, not a one-time sequence. Governance supports the others throughout the AI system’s life.

## A LaxAIHub example
For a proposed AI quiz helper, identify an owner, use approved learning material, check answer quality, label limitations, and provide a way to report errors. Review it after changes.

## Try it on paper
Write a one-page plan for that quiz helper: purpose, owner, permitted data, risks, tests, and what to do after a mistake.

**Remember: Responsible AI needs people who own the decisions and keep reviewing the results.**

Show a quiz helper surrounded by a responsible owner, approved data, quality checks, and feedback. Present Govern, Map, Measure, Manage as connected ongoing work.
Suggested pacing: 10 seconds for the everyday analogy, 25 seconds for the concept, 25 seconds for the access example, and 10 seconds for the takeaway.

## Paper lab
Draft a fictional AI use-case card: purpose, owner, users, data, risks, evaluation method, review date, and incident contact. This exercise is not a compliance certification.
Record the request, relevant evidence, decision, and reason. No accounts, configuration changes, or paid software are needed.
