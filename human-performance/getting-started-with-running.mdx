You do not need to run fast to become a runner. A comfortable beginning can be a mixture of walking and gentle jogging.

This article is an adult beginner introduction. Children should enjoy age-appropriate active play; an adult training plan is not automatically suitable for them.

## Start with a walk
Choose a safe, even route and comfortable footwear. Begin with walking before trying short jogging intervals.

The NHS Couch to 5K plan uses walking and running together, with three sessions a week and a rest day between runs. It progresses toward 30 minutes of running over nine weeks, but taking longer is fine. The time goal is not a guarantee of covering five kilometres.

## Follow a gradual plan
Use the official NHS plan for the detailed intervals. Keep the pace manageable, repeat a week if needed, and allow recovery. Walking is a worthwhile option on its own.

If you are unsure whether running is suitable because of your health, an injury, or symptoms, get advice from a healthcare professional first. Stop if you feel pain or become unwell. Chest pain, fainting, or severe breathing difficulty needs urgent medical attention.

## Try a reflection note
After an easy walk or suitable planned session, record what you did, how it felt, and when you will rest. Compare your own experience over time, rather than comparing speeds with others.

**Remember: A gradual, comfortable start is progress.**
