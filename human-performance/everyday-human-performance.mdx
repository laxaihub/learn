Human performance is not only about winning races. In this article, it means having the energy, attention, and physical ability to do everyday things that matter to you.

## Movement counts
Walking, wheeling, dancing, playing, and household activity all count as movement. Start from what you can comfortably do. Small amounts are useful; you do not need an intense workout to begin.

For adults, WHO recommends 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity a week, or an equivalent amount of vigorous activity, plus muscle-strengthening activity on at least two days. These are public-health goals to build toward, rather than a first-day challenge. Older adults also benefit from activity that develops balance and strength.

Children need age-appropriate active play and movement, rather than copying an adult workout. WHO recommends an average of at least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity daily across the week for ages 5–17.

## Recovery matters
Sleep supports attention and memory. Needs vary with age: adults aged 18–60 generally need at least seven hours; children aged 6–12 need nine to twelve hours.

## Try a small habit
Choose a manageable movement break and a regular bedtime routine. For a week, note what you did and how you felt. Your notes help you reflect; they do not prove that one habit caused a change.

**Remember: Progress can mean a steadier routine, not a faster score.**
