Strength helps with everyday tasks such as getting out of a chair and carrying things. You can begin learning simple movements without a gym.

This is a brief adult starter, not a complete whole-body program. If you have an injury, health concerns, or are unsure these movements suit you, ask a healthcare professional first. Children need age-appropriate activity with adult support.

## Prepare your space
Use a solid wall, a stable chair without wheels, and a clear floor. Warm up with easy movement. Breathe normally and stay within a comfortable range.

## Try three movements
**Sit to stand:** Start seated with your feet flat. Lean forward slightly, rise steadily, then lower yourself slowly. Use support if needed.

**Wall push-up:** Place your palms on a wall around chest height. Bend your elbows gently to move toward it, then push back. Keep your body controlled.

**Supported calf raise:** Hold a stable support, lift your heels comfortably, and lower them slowly.

For an easy first exploration, try up to five controlled repetitions of each, doing fewer if needed and resting between movements. This small practice is not a substitute for a tailored strength program. The NHS source below provides fuller demonstrations and routines.

Stop if you feel pain or become unwell. Chest pain, fainting, or severe breathing difficulty needs urgent medical attention.

## Notice your progress
Record whether each movement felt comfortable and controlled. Add difficulty gradually rather than chasing exhaustion.

**Remember: Comfortable control comes before more repetitions.**
